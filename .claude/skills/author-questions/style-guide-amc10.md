# AMC 10 Question Style Guide

_Distilled from the real 2022-2024 AMC 10 A/B exams (AoPS wiki archive). Bank content must be original._

# House Style: Original AMC 10 Practice Set (Hard Rules)

**Governing frame.** 25 problems, 75 minutes, no calculator, scoring 6 / 1.5 / 0. Every problem must be solvable in 1–8 minutes by hand once the key idea is seen. Difficulty lives in insight density, casework, and verification burden — never in long arithmetic. Every problem MUST be: (1) fully solvable from TEXT ALONE — no diagrams, tables, or figures; geometry stems must completely determine the configuration in prose, and constructing one's own picture is part of the intended difficulty; (2) ORIGINAL — archetypes may echo real exams, but numbers, setting, and the specific insight chain must be new, never a near-copy of any MAA problem; (3) calculator-free with every constant engineered for clean exact work (composed percents that divide evenly, Pythagorean triples, semiprime targets like 3·43, radicals that simplify, factorization-load-bearing year constants).

**Stem voice.** Declarative setup sentences first, then EXACTLY ONE terminal interrogative: "What is…", "How many…", "For how many…", "In how many ways…". Never imperative ("Find", "Compute" are banned), never mid-stem questions. Band 1: 1–3 sentences. Longer stems are permitted only to define a procedure or novel rule, never for scene-setting; in the back half, short means deep. Objects arrive pre-typed ("Positive integers $x$ and $y$ satisfy…", "Let $N$ be…"); every domain is pinned (positive, integer, distinct, ordered, nonzero). Extremal asks are explicit ("least possible value of"). Any term beyond core curriculum gets a parenthetical gloss ("lattice points (points both of whose coordinates are integers)"); floor notation is always defined in-stem. Any invented term is italicized, defined in one sentence, and immediately anchored with "(For example, …)" showing one concrete instance — for unusual conditions, verify the example against the condition. Close every loophole with a rider ("There were no ties.", "Outcomes are independent.", "uniformly at random"). Stems never coach: no "Note that", no method hints.

**Story vs. abstract, by band.** Q1–10: roughly half thin-story, half abstract. Stories are one named first-name character (culturally varied, one per problem, alphabetical initials if several map to labels), performing one clean action, with zero unused numbers. Q11–20: story fraction drops to 2–3 of 10, and a story appears only as the delivery vehicle for a novel rule or constraint system. Q21–25: fully abstract or task-framed (cards, strings, solids); no character names except, at most, one narrative that exists solely to deliver a perturbation.

**Reasoning-step targets (relative to THIS contest).** Q1–10: 2–4 genuine decisions — one real insight plus clean execution; nothing is autopilot, even Q1 hides a structural observation; most are backsolvable. Q11–20: 4–6 steps in phases — a representation change the stem never mentions, systematic execution, then a mandatory filter (integrality, positivity, feasibility, membership) that kills the naive branch and whose skipped version lands on a listed distractor; at least half fuse two topic domains; include one knowledge-gated short problem and one grind-gated case problem. Q21–25 are near-AIME: either 6+ steps with real error surface, or a short chain gated behind a non-obvious reframe (the reframe IS the problem); require building a representation (coordinates, identity, valuation, symmetry argument) before computing, and use an answer-encoding wrapper — "can be written as $\frac{m}{n}$ where $m$ and $n$ are relatively prime positive integers; what is $m+n$?", "sum of the digits of $N$", "what is $a+b+c$?" — with normalization stated, so option-testing and estimation recover nothing. House phrase is "relatively prime", never "coprime".

**Choices.** Exactly five, one inline math line: `$\textbf{(A) } v\qquad\textbf{(B) } v\qquad\dots$`, numeric values in ascending order, fractions fully reduced, radicals simplified, no decimals unless units force them, no "none of the above". One verbal option per set at most (`\text{infinitely many}`), placed last. All five choices share the answer's type, form, and magnitude family so type-checking eliminates nothing. Correct answers must land on all five letters across the set, including extremes. Spacing signals difficulty: wide only when estimation is the tested skill; consecutive-integer ladders for counts and encoded answers; tight clusters late.

**Distractor engineering.** Every wrong choice is the reachable terminus of a nameable defective path — never noise. Draw from: the naive/uncorrected answer (mandatory in Q1–10); subexpression values and error-propagated completions; partial-case sums for enumerations; each specific modeling slip propagated through the correct downstream pipeline; symmetry corrections done wrong (÷k for ÷k!); the skipped-verification branch; adjacent-question answers in multi-group accounting (the "read the question twice" trap); off-by-one windows (±1, ±2) around the truth; for "how many of the following" questions, the full range 0–n with the natural partial-analysis stopping point on a middle option. In Q21–25 the correct answer may be the formally ugliest option.

**Notation.** Every numeral in math mode, even mid-prose ("$5$ bins", "$40\%$", "$3\times3$ grid"). Currency/units in words in stems, never bare $ (use `\$`inside math for money). Focal equations get display`\[…\]`. Thousands separators `1{,}700`. Geometry: cyclic vertex labels ($ABCD$), $\overline{BC}$, $\angle BAC$, $\triangle ABC$, "Point $M$ lies on $\overline{XY}$", ratios as $\tfrac{BC}{AD}$. Sequences `$1, 2, \ldots, n$`; `\cdot` for multiplication. Thread one engineered year-like constant (with load-bearing factorization) through 2–4 problems; an occasional decoy datum in the middle band is allowed once, and it must be recognizably discardable.
